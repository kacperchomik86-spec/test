Little Catechism on Sedevacantism (Totalism)


✦ ✦ ✦

1. What is "sedevacantism"?

"Sedevacantism" is the theological position of traditional Catholics who profess that the Apostolic See is currently vacant. This position is rooted in Catholic dogmatic teaching concerning the infallibility, visibility, and indefectibility of the Church.


2. What does the term "Sede Vacante" mean?
The term "Sede Vacante" is a Latin phrase meaning "the See being vacant." It refers specifically to the canonical period during which the Papal Chair lacks a valid, living occupant to exercise papal jurisdiction.
3.  Does sedevacantism reject the Papacy or the Church?
Sedevacantism does not reject the Papacy or the Church, but rather defends papal infallibility and ecclesial indefectibility by repudiating illegitimate claimants who profess public heresy. By refusing allegiance to false shepherds, traditional Catholics preserve intact the divine constitution of the Supreme Pontificate.
4. Can a true Pope teach false doctrine or promulgate harmful universal laws?
A true Pope cannot teach false doctrine or promulgate harmful universal laws, because the Holy See is preserved from error by divine assistance through the Church's infallible Ordinary Magisterium. The Vicar of Christ can never impose sacrilegious rites, bad discipline, or heretical decrees upon the universal Church.
5. What happens if a person holding the papal office becomes a public heretic?
If a person holding the papal office becomes a public heretic, he automatically ceases to be a member and the visible head of the Catholic Church. As Saint Robert Bellarmine teaches, one who is no longer a member of the Mystical Body cannot remain its supreme head.
6. Why is the Apostolic See vacant today?
The Apostolic See is vacant today because the post-conciliar occupants publicly professed modernist heresies and promulgated harmful liturgical rites and non-Catholic universal laws. By officially imposing the errors of Vatican II, these claimants severed themselves from the Roman Catholic Church.
7. Does a heretical pope lose his office ipso facto without needing a declaratory sentence?
A heretical pope loses his office ipso facto without needing a declaratory sentence, operating by divine law (jure divino) through tacit resignation (tacitam resignationem) under Canon 188.4 of the 1917 Code of Canon Law upon public defection from the faith. As Saint Robert Bellarmine and universal tradition teach, a public heretic is divested of all authority and jurisdiction per se.
8.  Can a public heretic be validly elected to the Papacy?
A public heretic cannot be validly elected to the Papacy, as such an election is null, invalid, and void (nulla, irrita et inania) eo ipso by divine law. This principle was explicitly confirmed by Pope Paul IV in his Apostolic Constitution Cum ex Apostolatus Officio.
9. How does sedevacantism differ from the "recognize and resist" position of the FSSPX?
Sedevacantism differs from the "recognize and resist" position of the Society of Saint Pius X (FSSPX) by completely rejecting the authority of heretical claimants to the Papacy. Conversely, the FSSPX recognizes these claimants as valid Popes while sifting and resisting their official universal laws, liturgical rites, and magisterial teaching.
10. Why is the "recognize and resist" stance untenable for a Catholic?
The "recognize and resist" stance is untenable for a Catholic because it sets up a private society as a surrogate Magisterium (surogat Magisterium) to sift papal acts and reject solemn, infallible canonizations. Furthermore, it violates dogmatic Catholic teaching regarding obligatory submission to the Roman Pontiff and the infallibility of universal discipline.
11. Does St. Robert Bellarmine’s teaching on resisting a bad pope justify resisting modern bad laws?
Saint Robert Bellarmine’s teaching on resisting a bad pope does not justify resisting modern bad universal laws, liturgical rites, or magisterial decrees. His teaching applied strictly to resisting morally bad individual commands (praecepta) or tyrannical physical aggression, not to opposing universal ecclesiastical laws (leges) or binding doctrinal judgments.
12. How does total sedevacantism differ from the Thesis of Cassiciacum?
Total sedevacantism differs from the Thesis of Cassiciacum because total sedevacantism holds the Apostolic See to be completely vacant both formally and materially (formaliter et materialiter). In contrast, the Thesis of Cassiciacum posits that the post-conciliar claimant remains a pope "materially" (materialiter tantum) while lacking formal papal authority.
12.  How can the Church remain indefectible during a long vacancy of the Holy See?
The Church can remain indefectible during a long vacancy of the Holy See because the Catholic Faith, the valid episcopate, and the essential means of grace endure intact without alteration. The divine promise guarantees that the gates of hell shall never prevail against the Church, even when the supreme shepherd is absent.
12.  How can traditional priests lawfully administer sacraments without local diocesan faculties?
Traditional priests can lawfully administer sacraments without local diocesan faculties under the principle of canonical equity (epikeia) and supplied jurisdiction (jurisdictio suppleta). They act lawfully because in a state of necessity the salvation of souls is the supreme law of the Church (salus animarum suprema lex).
13.  Must a Catholic reject the Novus Ordo Mass and the doctrines of Vatican II?
A Catholic must reject the Novus Ordo Mass and the doctrines of Vatican II because they compromise the divine faith and introduce dangerous, heterodox errors harmful to souls. The new rites and conciliar decrees represent a departure from Catholic Tradition already condemned by the Magisterium.
13.   May a traditional Catholic assist at a Mass celebrated "una cum" a false post-conciliar pope?
A traditional Catholic may not assist at a Mass offered una cum (in union with) a false post-conciliar pope. Reciting or affirming the una cum in the Canon falsely professes communion in faith and government with an illegitimate, heretical authority.
14.   What is the fundamental duty of a Catholic in this present state of necessity?
The fundamental duty of a Catholic in this present state of necessity is to preserve the divine Catholic faith uncompromised, frequent valid traditional sacraments, and refuse all communion with modernism and false heretical hierarchies. By remaining steadfast in perpetual Tradition, the faithful fulfill the divine command to work out their salvation.
